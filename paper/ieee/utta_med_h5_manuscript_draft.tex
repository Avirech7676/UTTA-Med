\begin{abstract}
Deep learning models trained on source pathology centers frequently degrade when deployed across unseen hospitals and optical slide scanners. Test-time adaptation (TTA) aims to mitigate distribution shift using unlabeled target streams, but unconstrained entropy minimization (Tent) is notoriously unstable: on Camelyon17-WILDS multicenter metastasis classification, Tent collapses on 2 of 5 random seeds ($F_1 \approx 0.05$, harmful adaptation rate $18.7\% \pm 17.9\%$). In this work, we demonstrate that this catastrophic failure is driven by single-class attractor collapse on histochemical outliers (documented on Slide 29, where Tent AUROC plunges from $0.8592 \to 0.0521$). We formulate \textbf{UTTA-MED}, coupling \textbf{Entropic Optimal Transport prototype alignment (OT-TTA)} with \textbf{fail-closed runtime safeguards (SafeAdapt)}, evaluated across multicenter lymph node classification (Camelyon17-WILDS, $N=85{,}054$ patches across 10 whole-slide images; 9 patients) and dense mitotic figure detection (MIDOG 2021, 50 held-out cases, 1,100 patches across scanner shifts). On Camelyon17 Hospital~2, by strictly enforcing marginal class balance ($\mathbf{Q}^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$) via Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations, OT-TTA mathematically precludes majority-class attractor collapse, reversing negative transfer on Slide 29 ($0.8592 \to \mathbf{0.9324} \pm 0.0078$ mean AUROC; $0.9360$ on seed 42) and elevating cohort performance to $\mathbf{0.9495} \pm 0.0134$ macro-AUROC across five random seeds ($p = 0.0312$, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test), cutting Expected Calibration Error by 43.2\% ($\text{ECE} = 0.0745$), and slashing harmful adaptation to $\mathbf{0.0018} \pm 0.0003$. We analytically prove via first-order Taylor expansion (Proposition 1) that Monte Carlo Dropout predictive variance and output confidence are rank-equivalent on binary classification ($\rho = -0.9917$, $p = 9.73 \times 10^{-286}$), establishing why scalar uncertainty filtering fails to prevent confirmation bias. For dense mitotic figure detection, we introduce the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR), which conditions silence monitoring on hematoxylin optical density, eliminating all 14 false silence alarms on benign acellular stroma and enabling continuous active adaptation ($F_1 = \mathbf{0.5160} \pm 0.0050$, $\Delta F_1 = +0.0575$, $p < 0.001$). Finally, we prove a PAC $L_\infty$ drift bound (Theorem 1) guaranteeing that parameter projection ($\|\theta_t - \theta_0\|_\infty \le 0.05$) bounds maximum output probability displacement to $\le 0.0460$. These findings demonstrate that geometry-preserving transport alignment and fail-closed runtime interlocks provide essential reliability containment under severe histopathological domain shift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{ross-center} distribution shift is an unavoidable operational impediment in clinical computational pathology: differences in whole-slide scanner optics, sensor spectral responses, histochemical staining protocols, microtomy section thickness, and patient cohorts induce substantial domain divergence~\cite{koh2021wilds,bandi2018camelyon17,aubreville2023mitosis}. A deep neural network trained to high diagnostic accuracy on source institutions frequently retains discriminative ranking in-domain while suffering severe drops in sensitivity, calibration, and patient-level staging accuracy upon deployment at an unseen hospital~\cite{histopathc2026}.

Test-time adaptation (TTA) addresses this diagnostic gap by updating model parameters online using unlabeled target batches during clinical inference~\cite{wang2021tent,liang2023comprehensive}. Unsupervised entropy minimization (Tent)~\cite{wang2021tent} has gained widespread adoption due to its modular simplicity: optimizing prediction entropy over Batch Normalization affine scale and shift parameters ($\gamma, \beta$) without accessing source data. However, entropy minimization operates under an unverified premise: that low-entropy target predictions are predominantly correct. When histochemical or optical shifts violate this premise, high-confidence incorrect pseudo-labels act as reinforcement signals, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop that causes catastrophic feature representation collapse~\cite{histopathc2026}.

In this work, we conduct a systematic, reproducible \textbf{reliability and failure-containment investigation} across two distinct operational regimes in digital pathology:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Multicenter Metastasis Classification:} High-throughput binary patch classification on Camelyon17-WILDS~\cite{koh2021wilds}, where dense tissue morphology is evaluated across five clinical centers ($N=85{,}054$ patches across 10 WSIs from 9 patients).
\item \textbf{Dense Mitotic Figure Detection:} Spatially sparse object detection on MIDOG 2021~\cite{aubreville2023mitosis}, where rare cellular divisions (mitoses) must be localized across diverse slide-scanning hardware (50 held-out cases, 1,100 patches).
\end{enumerate}

We test a precise operational hypothesis: \emph{can geometry-preserving transport alignment and fail-closed runtime interlocks contain catastrophic TTA failure modes while achieving statistically verified positive adaptation?}

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Geometry-Preserving Optimal Transport (OT-TTA):} To resolve the root cause of confirmation bias and single-class attractor collapse under stain shift, we formulate test-time adaptation as an Entropic Optimal Transport (Wasserstein-2) problem against preserved source class prototypes via Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations ($\min_{Q \in \Pi} \langle Q, C \rangle - \epsilon H(Q)$). By strictly enforcing marginal class balance ($Q^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$), OT-TTA mathematically precludes majority-class collapse, reversing negative transfer on Camelyon17 outlier Slide 29 ($0.8592 \to \mathbf{0.9324}$ mean AUROC; $0.9360$ on seed 42; Hospital~2 macro-AUROC $\mathbf{0.9495} \pm 0.0134$, $p = 0.0312$).
\item \textbf{Analytical Equivalence Proof on Binary Heads:} We provide a formal mathematical proof via first-order Taylor expansion (Proposition 1) explaining why binary MC-Dropout predictive variance is asymptotically rank-equivalent to output confidence ($\rho = -0.9917$). This establishes theoretically why scalar uncertainty filtering fails to prevent confirmation bias and motivates geometric prototype alignment.
\item \textbf{Biologically Grounded Detection Safeguards (CMR):} On MIDOG 2021 multi-scanner mitosis detection, we decouple legitimate absence of mitoses in acellular stroma from detector collapse via the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR), eliminating all 14 false silence alarms across the 1,100-patch stream and achieving stable positive adaptation ($F_1 = \mathbf{0.5160} \pm 0.0050$, $\Delta F_1 = +0.0575$, $p < 0.001$).
\item \textbf{PAC $L_\infty$ Drift Bound and Atomic Interlocks:} We prove that projecting parameters within an affine trust region ($\|\theta_t - \theta_0\|_\infty \le 0.05$) bounds maximum output probability shift to $\le 0.0460$ (Theorem 1). We couple this with sub-frame dual-buffer atomic state rollbacks ($1.8$\,ms) and episodic per-WSI resets ensuring patient diagnostic isolation.
\item \textbf{Exploratory Patient-Level Staging Case Study:} Evaluating pathological nodal staging (AJCC 8th Edition) across all 10 WSIs of Hospital 2, we document that unconstrained Tent collapses staging accuracy from 88.0\% to 50.0\% by missing macrometastases on outlier slides, whereas UTTA-MED preserves 86.0\% diagnostic concordance.
\end{enumerate}

Rather than making unverified claims of unconstrained state-of-the-art gains, we demonstrate that UTTA-MED provides a principled geometry-preserving pathway that overcomes confirmation bias, surrounded by verified fail-closed runtime bounds.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Test-Time Adaptation in Vision vs.\ Digital Pathology.}
Tent updates BatchNorm affine parameters ($\gamma, \beta$) via prediction entropy minimization~\cite{wang2021tent}, but remains susceptible to catastrophic collapse on out-of-distribution outliers. EATA introduces sample entropy thresholding and Fisher divergence penalties to reduce forgetting~\cite{niu2022eata}, yet cannot rollback after contaminated batches. SAR stabilizes adaptation using sharpness-aware minimization and reverts parameters upon isolated batch gradient norm spikes~\cite{niu2023sar}; however, gradient spikes do not capture slow histochemical prediction collapse. CoTTA proposes continual adaptation with stochastic weight restoration~\cite{wang2022cotta}, and RoTTA utilizes category-balanced memory banks~\cite{yuan2023rotta}. MEMO optimizes marginal prediction entropy across multiple augmented views of individual samples~\cite{zhang2022memo}; however, test-time augmentations cannot invert non-linear chromatic stain rotations and incur prohibitive $16\times$ computational latency ($>500$\,ms/patch). In computational pathology, memory banks violate cross-patient data isolation and privacy regulations (HIPAA/GDPR), while continual drift accumulates unbounded error across heterogeneous Whole-Slide Images (WSIs). SafeAdapt enforces episodic resets per WSI, ensuring strict patient diagnostic independence.

\textbf{Medical TTA, Generative Normalization, and Foundation Models.}
In medical imaging, CertainTTA integrates predictive and epistemic uncertainty for segmentation~\cite{certaintta2025}, while SicTTA explores single-image continual segmentation~\cite{sictta2026}. Generative approaches such as TT-SaD~\cite{ttsad2024} and AdaPath~\cite{chen2023adapath} perform test-time stain transfer via CycleGAN or diffusion; however, generative normalization introduces substantial computational latency ($100$--$300$\,ms/patch) and hallucination artifacts. In contrast, UTTA-MED performs direct parameter adaptation under strict $L_\infty$ parameter trust regions without heavy generative overhead.

\textbf{Distribution-Free Uncertainty and Conformal Risk Control.}
Conformal prediction provides rigorous, distribution-free risk guarantees~\cite{bates2023conformal}. While static conformal prediction produces prediction sets without parameter updates, SafeAdapt incorporates Split Conformal Risk Control into the TTA admission policy, providing finite-sample error rate bounds.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let $\mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ denote the input space of digitized histopathology image patches, and let $\mathcal{Y}$ denote the label space ($\mathcal{Y} = \{0, 1\}$ for binary lymph node metastasis classification, and $\mathcal{Y} = \{(b_k, c_k)\}_{k=1}^m$ with bounding boxes $b_k \in \mathbb{R}^4$ for mitotic figure detection). We are given a source model $f_{\theta_0}$ pre-trained on labelled dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{source}} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_s}$ sampled from source distribution $P_{\text{source}}(X, Y)$.

During deployment, the model receives an unlabeled sequential target stream $\mathcal{S}_{\text{target}} = \{x_t\}_{t=1}^T$ drawn from shifted distribution $P_{\text{target}}(X)$ ($P_{\text{target}}(X) \ne P_{\text{source}}(X)$). We operate under the standard online test-time adaptation setting:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Target labels $\{y_t\}$ are strictly unavailable during adaptation.
\item Model updates must occur sequentially using streaming batches of size $B$.
\item Backpropagation through large feature backbones is restricted to preserve clinical inference latency.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Base Architecture and Trainable Parameters}
For patch classification, let $f_\theta$ be a ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} parameterized by $\theta$, outputting logit $z(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ with sigmoid probability $p(x) = \sigma(z(x))$. For object detection, let $f_\theta$ be a Faster R-CNN~\cite{ren2015fasterrcnn} with a ResNet-50 Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) backbone. Following the standard BatchNorm TTA protocol~\cite{wang2021tent}, all convolutional, linear, and backbone feature weights remain strictly frozen. In the detection backbone, torchvision utilizes \texttt{FrozenBatchNorm2d} modules by default; affine adaptation is enabled by promoting the scale $\gamma$ and shift $\beta$ buffers of all 53 normalization layers (106 parameters) into trainable parameters with \texttt{requires\_grad=True}. Crucially, running mean and variance buffers remain frozen at their source-trained values (\texttt{bn\_freeze\_stats}), preventing small or non-stationary target batches from corrupting global normalization statistics. Optimization uses Adam ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-5}$) with a single inner gradient step per batch ($k=1$) for classification, and SGD ($\mathrm{lr}=2.5\times 10^{-4}, \text{momentum}=0.9$) for detection.

\subsection{Selective Uncertainty Gating (Classification)}
Dropout ($p=0.5$) in the classification head remains active at test time. For each target patch $x$, $N=20$ stochastic forward passes are computed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mc_dropout}
\bar{p}(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N p_j(x), \quad
U(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \bigl(p_j(x) - \bar{p}(x)\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
The number of passes $N=20$ was chosen based on validation experiments ($N \in \{5, 10, 20, 30, 50\}$), where the correlation between $U(x)$ and prediction error plateaued at $N=20$ ($r=0.425$ vs.\ $0.437$ at $N=50$) while saving $2.5\times$ computational overhead. A binary hard gate decides whether patch $x$ is admitted to adaptation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gating}
w(x) = \mathbf{1}\bigl[U(x) < \tau\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is set to the 70th percentile of $U$ computed on held-out validation Hospital~1. Target hospital labels are strictly quarantined and never used for threshold selection, learning rate tuning, or model selection. If an entire mini-batch is rejected ($\sum_i w_i = 0$), the optimization step is skipped entirely. Otherwise, the model updates via entropy minimization over admitted patches:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:entropy_loss}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{\sum_i w_i H(p_i)}{\sum_i w_i}, \quad
H(p) = -p \log p - (1-p) \log (1-p).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Theoretical Equivalence in Binary Classification and PAC Drift Bounds}
\label{sec:theory}
While MC-Dropout variance $U(x)$ captures epistemic uncertainty under stochastic weight perturbations, on single-logit binary classification tasks $U(x)$ is asymptotically rank-equivalent to standard output confidence.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (Invariant Permutation Ranking).} \emph{Let $p(x) = \sigma(f(x))$ and confidence $C(x) = \max(p(x), 1 - p(x))$. Binary Shannon entropy $H(p) = -p \ln p - (1-p) \ln(1-p)$ satisfies $\frac{dH}{dC} = -|f(x)| < 0$ for all $p \ne 0.5$. Thus, $-H(p)$ is a strictly monotonic transformation of $C(x)$, inducing an identical sample permutation ranking $\pi_{\text{entropy}} \equiv \pi_{\text{confidence}}$ across any target dataset.}

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Epistemic Variance Asymptotics).} \emph{Under Gaussian weight perturbations $w \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_w, \Sigma_w)$ induced by Dropout, the predictive variance admits the first-order Taylor expansion $U(x) \approx [p(x)(1 - p(x))]^2 \cdot \phi(x)^T \Sigma_w \phi(x)$. For bounded feature representations $\phi(x)$, $U(x)$ is predominantly governed by $[p(1-p)]^2$, which strictly peaks at $p = 0.5$ and decreases monotonically in $|p - 0.5|$. Consequently, epistemic variance ranking $\pi_{\text{variance}}$ and confidence ranking $\pi_{\text{confidence}}$ are asymptotically equivalent ($\rho = -0.9917$).}

\textbf{Assumption 1 (Feature Compactness).} \emph{Pre-trained convolutional feature representations lie within a bounded compact ball in Euclidean norm: $\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \|\phi(x)\|_2 \le R_\phi < \infty$.}

\textbf{Assumption 2 (Head Regularity).} \emph{The linear classification head $f(x) = W^T \phi(x) + b$ has bounded matrix spectral norm (operator 2-norm) $\|W\|_2 \le L_W$. The sigmoid activation mapping satisfies $|\sigma'(z)| \le 1/4$ for all $z \in \mathbb{R}$.}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Bounded Parameter Drift and Probability Perturbation).} \emph{Let affine normalization parameters $\theta = (\gamma, \beta)$ be constrained to an $L_\infty$ ball of radius $\epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}$ centered at source weights $\theta_0$, while linear classification head weights remain fixed. If parameter projection $\|\theta_t - \theta_0\|_\infty \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}$ is enforced at each step $t \in [1, T]$, then for any target sample $x \in \mathcal{X}$ across the entire adaptation trajectory $t \in [1, T]$, the maximum output probability shift from the source baseline satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theorem1_bound}
\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}, t \in [1, T]} |\sigma(z_t(x)) - \sigma(z_0(x))| \le \frac{1}{4} L_W R_\phi \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{equation}
For empirical source weights with operator norm $\|W_{\mathrm{head}}\|_2 = 1.8421$ and normalized feature radius $R_\phi \le 2.0$, maximum output probability deviation cannot exceed $\Delta p_{\max} \le 0.0460$. Consequently, single-class attractor collapse ($p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$) is mathematically precluded across the full adaptation trajectory for all samples with initial baseline margin $|p_0(x) - 0.5| > 0.0460$.}

\subsection{Geometry-Preserving Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA)}
\label{sec:ot_tta}
Conventional test-time adaptation methods (e.g., Tent, EATA, SAR) optimize point-wise Shannon prediction entropy $\sum_k -p_k \log p_k$. In digital pathology, cross-center staining divergence operates as a systematic chromatic transformation. Because point-wise entropy is indifferent to global class proportions, streaming patches are easily attracted into a self-reinforcing majority-class feedback loop, precipitating single-class attractor collapse (empirically documented on Camelyon17 Slide~29; Fig.~\ref{fig:slide29_reversal}).

To eliminate this fundamental flaw, we formulate test-time adaptation as an Entropic Optimal Transport (Wasserstein-2) problem against preserved source class prototypes $\mathcal{P}_{\text{source}} = \{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\mu}_K\}$, where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \frac{1}{|S_k|} \sum_{x \in S_k} \frac{\phi(x)}{\|\phi(x)\|_2}$ denotes the $L_2$-normalized source centroid for class $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$. For a streaming target batch of $B$ representations $\{\phi(x_i)\}_{i=1}^B$, we construct the cosine distance transport cost matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times K}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_cost}
C_{ik} = 1 - \frac{\phi(x_i)^T \boldsymbol{\mu}_k}{\|\phi(x_i)\|_2 \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2}.
\end{equation}
The optimal soft assignment coupling $\mathbf{Q}^* \in \mathbb{R}_+^{B \times K}$ solves the entropic regularized transport problem over the transportation polytope $\Pi(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_objective}
\min_{\mathbf{Q} \in \Pi(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})} \langle \mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{C} \rangle - \epsilon_{\text{ot}} H(\mathbf{Q}),
\end{equation}
subject to the exact marginal constraints:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_marginals}
\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{1}_K = \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{B} \mathbf{1}_B, \quad \mathbf{Q}^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{r},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r} \in \Delta^K$ denotes the certified source class prior distribution ($\sum_k r_k = 1.0$), and $H(\mathbf{Q}) = -\sum_{i, k} Q_{ik} (\ln Q_{ik} - 1)$.

By the Sinkhorn-Knopp theorem, the unique optimal transport coupling takes the scaling form $\mathbf{Q}^* = \text{diag}(\mathbf{u}^*) \mathbf{K} \text{diag}(\mathbf{v}^*)$, where kernel $\mathbf{K} = \exp(-\mathbf{C} / \epsilon_{\text{ot}})$. The dual scaling vectors $\mathbf{u}^*$ and $\mathbf{v}^*$ are computed via efficient matrix-vector iterations ($O(B \cdot K)$ per step):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sinkhorn_iters}
\mathbf{u}^{(t+1)} = \frac{\frac{1}{B} \mathbf{1}_B}{\mathbf{K} \mathbf{v}^{(t)}}, \quad \mathbf{v}^{(t+1)} = \frac{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{K}^T \mathbf{u}^{(t+1)}},
\end{equation}
converging deterministically within $<20$ iterations at $\epsilon_{\text{ot}} = 0.05$.

The total adaptation objective combines the OT prototype transport loss, an anti-attractor Kullback-Leibler diversity penalty against class priors, and an $L_2$ parameter anchor:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_total_loss}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \lambda_{\text{ot}} \sum_{i=1}^B \sum_{k=1}^K Q^*_{ik} C_{ik} + \lambda_{\text{div}} D_{\text{KL}}(\bar{\mathbf{p}} \,\|\, \mathbf{r}) + \lambda_{\text{anchor}} \|\theta - \theta_0\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{p}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \mathbf{p}(x_i)$, $\lambda_{\text{ot}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{div}} = 0.5$, and $\lambda_{\text{anchor}} = 0.01$. Because constraint~(\ref{eq:ot_marginals}) enforces $\mathbf{Q}^{*T} \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$, the target batch is mathematically prohibited from assigning all samples to a single dominant class, precluding attractor collapse and preserving class geometry under chromatic drift.

\subsection{Parameter-Space Trust Regions and Atomic Rollback Mechanics}
\label{sec:trust_region_mechanics}
To prevent gradual unconstrained parameter drift during online adaptation, SafeAdapt confines all trainable parameters $\theta$ (the Batch Normalization affine scales $\gamma$ and shifts $\beta$) within a strictly bounded $L_\infty$ trust region centered at certified source parameters $\theta_0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:trust_clip}
\theta_t = \text{clip}\bigl(\theta_t, \theta_0 - \epsilon_{\text{trust}}, \theta_0 + \epsilon_{\text{trust}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\text{trust}} = 0.05$. By Theorem~1, this projection bounds maximum output probability displacement to $\Delta p \le 0.0460$, ensuring that confident baseline predictions cannot flip into false attractors under histochemical shift.

Furthermore, SafeAdapt maintains dual asynchronous parameter ring buffers: a high-speed GPU state snapshot $\mathcal{B}_{\text{GPU}}$ and an immutable host CPU anchor $\mathcal{B}_{\text{CPU}} = \theta_0$. If an empirical anomaly is detected (e.g., a batch gradient norm spike $\|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}\|_2 > \tau_{\text{grad}}$ or consecutive collapse alarms), an atomic rollback is triggered:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rollback_op}
\theta \leftarrow \mathcal{B}_{\text{GPU}}, \quad \Delta W = 0.00000000.
\end{equation}
Profiling on dedicated GPU hardware confirms that this memory copy completes in $1.8$\,ms ($<4\%$ of a standard $53.5$\,ms inference forward pass), executing within the inter-batch latency budget. If five consecutive rollbacks occur within a single Whole-Slide Image, SafeAdapt permanently latches global fallback: the model locks to certified source state $\theta_0$ for the remainder of that slide. Upon detecting the boundary of a new slide, model parameters and optimizer momentum buffers are deterministically reset to $\theta_0$, guaranteeing that patient diagnostic trajectories remain strictly isolated.

\subsection{Risk-Aware Selective Adaptation via Weighted Conformal Scoring}
\label{sec:conformal_risk}
Standard split conformal prediction guarantees $1-\alpha$ coverage under sample exchangeability~\cite{bates2023conformal}. However, in multicenter computational pathology, cross-hospital transfer violates exchangeability ($P_{\mathrm{target}}(X) \ne P_{\mathrm{calib}}(X)$, verified via Maximum Mean Discrepancy $\mathrm{MMD}^2 = 0.0418, p < 0.001$). To account for distribution shift, SafeAdapt employs Covariate-Shift Weighted Conformal Prediction~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal} with regularized importance weight truncation.

To prevent variance explosion in low-density calibration regions, importance weights are truncated at $B_w = 5.0$: $w_\beta(x) = \min(w(x), B_w)$. For non-conformity score $s(x, y) = 1 - f_{\hat{\theta}}(x)_y$, the shift-calibrated threshold $\hat{\lambda}_w$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted_conformal}
\hat{\lambda}_w = \inf \left\{ \lambda \in \mathbb{R} : \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{w_\beta(x_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n w_\beta(x_j) + w_\beta(x)} \mathbf{1}_{\{s(x_i, y_i) \le \lambda\}} \ge 1 - \alpha \right\}.
\end{equation}
An adaptation step is admitted if and only if $|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\lambda}_w}(x)| = 1$ (unambiguous singleton set). Because $w_\beta(x) \le B_w < \infty$, the estimator variance satisfies $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\lambda}_w) \le \frac{B_w^2}{4n}$.

\subsection{Cellularity-Aware Mitosis Detection Safety (CMR)}
\label{sec:cmr}
In dense object detection, mitotic figures are biologically rare. Evaluating silence over acellular stroma falsely trips naive counters. We define the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cmr}
\text{CMR}(x) = \frac{n_{\text{mitoses}}(x)}{\max(\rho_{\text{cell}}(x), \epsilon)}, \quad \rho_{\text{cell}}(x) = \frac{1}{|x|} \sum_{u,v} \mathbf{1}_{\{V_{\text{hem}}(u,v) > \tau_{\text{nuc}}\}}.
\end{equation}
The silence monitor increments its counter if and only if: (i) $n_{\text{pred}} = 0$, (ii) nuclear cellularity fraction $\rho_{\text{cell}}(x) \ge 0.15$ (hypercellular tumor), and (iii) source detector predicted mitosis ($n_{\text{source}} > 0$). This biological conditioning eliminates false silence alarms on benign stroma while capturing true detector collapse.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{SafeAdapt Runtime Safeguards with OT-TTA and CMR}
\label{alg:safeadapt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Certified source weights $\theta_0$, target stream $\{x_t\}_{t=1}^T$, class prototypes $\mathcal{P}_{\text{source}}$, trust radius $\epsilon_{\text{trust}} = 0.05$.
\STATE Initialize active weights $\theta \leftarrow \theta_0$, dual ring buffers $\mathcal{B}_{\text{CPU}}, \mathcal{B}_{\text{GPU}} \leftarrow \theta_0$.
\FOR{each streaming batch $\mathcal{B} = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^B$}
    \STATE Evaluate pre-adaptation gating:
    \IF{Classification}
        \STATE Compute conformal set $|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\lambda}_w}(x_i)|$ and uncertainty $U(x_i)$.
        \STATE Filter admitted subset $\mathcal{B}_{\text{admit}} = \{x_i : |\mathcal{C}(x_i)| = 1 \land U(x_i) < \tau\}$.
    \ELSIF{Dense Detection}
        \STATE Compute cellularity $\rho_{\text{cell}}(x_i)$ and CMR metric (Eq.~\ref{eq:cmr}).
        \STATE Check silence condition: $\mathbf{1}[n_{\text{pred}}=0 \land \rho_{\text{cell}} \ge 0.15 \land n_{\text{source}} > 0]$.
    \ENDIF
    \IF{Alarm tripped ($\ge 1$ anomaly)}
        \STATE Execute atomic parameter rollback: $\theta \leftarrow \mathcal{B}_{\text{GPU}}$ ($\Delta W = 0$ in $1.8$\,ms).
        \IF{Consecutive alarms $\ge 5$}
            \STATE Latch global fallback: lock $\theta \leftarrow \theta_0$ for remainder of WSI.
        \ENDIF
    \ELSE
        \STATE Snapshot current parameters into ring buffer: $\mathcal{B}_{\text{GPU}} \leftarrow \theta$.
        \STATE Compute transport plan $\mathbf{Q}^*$ via Sinkhorn iterations (Eq.~\ref{eq:sinkhorn_iters}).
        \STATE Perform gradient step on Eq.~(\ref{eq:ot_total_loss}): $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$.
        \STATE Project parameters to trust region: $\theta \leftarrow \text{clip}(\theta, \theta_0 - \epsilon_{\text{trust}}, \theta_0 + \epsilon_{\text{trust}})$.
    \ENDIF
    \IF{WSI boundary detected}
        \STATE Episodic Reset: flush optimizer momentum, restore $\theta \leftarrow \theta_0$.
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\item Target Test Cohort: Hospital~2 ($n=85{,}054$ patches across 10 WSIs; 9 patients).
\end{itemize}
Whole-slide image IDs are strictly partitioned across splits to avoid leakage.

\textbf{Random Seeds \& Metrics.} Five independent random seeds are evaluated: $\{42, 123, 2024, 7, 99\}$. We report AUROC, AUPRC, $F_1$ score, Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 15 bins)~\cite{guo2017calibration}, Brier score, adaptation coverage fraction, and the harmful adaptation rate:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:harm_rate}
\mathrm{Harm} = \frac{|\{i : y_i = \hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{before}} \land y_i \ne \hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{after}}\}|}{|\{i : y_i = \hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{before}}\}|}.
\end{equation}

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Failure Containment vs.\ Positive Domain Transfer.} Our empirical findings delineate two distinct operational regimes: \emph{defensive failure containment} and \emph{positive domain transfer}. In defensive containment, SafeAdapt functions as a certified fail-closed circuit breaker: upon detecting anomalies, atomic parameter rollbacks ($\Delta W = 0.00000000$ in $1.8$\,ms) and global source fallbacks guarantee that diagnostic performance cannot degrade below certified baseline levels. In positive adaptation, geometry-preserving prototype transport (OT-TTA) and biologically conditioned gating (CMR) transcend simple circuit breaking, achieving genuine diagnostic improvements while remaining strictly enveloped by fail-closed runtime bounds.

\textbf{Negative Transfer as a Clinical Containment Reality.} Real-world computational pathology frequently encounters severe domain outliers where adaptation without labels is fundamentally ill-posed. Under clinical quality assurance principles, autonomous diagnostic systems must exhibit deterministic fail-closed containment. SafeAdapt's value lies not in fabricating artificial target gains on intractable distributions, but in bounding parameter drift within an $L_\infty \le 0.05$ trust region and restoring certified source weights before patient staging is compromised.

\textbf{Safety Design via Deterministic Per-WSI Resets.} Adapting parameters continuously across patient streams introduces diagnostic non-stationarity: Patient B's diagnosis becomes a function of Patient A's tissue morphology. SafeAdapt resolves this by formulating adaptation as an episodic process with deterministic resets: model weights and optimizer buffers are flushed to certified source state $\theta_0$ upon whole-slide image boundaries, guaranteeing permutation invariance across patient arrival sequences and ensuring strict patient diagnostic isolation.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Target Cohort Patient Volume:} While patch volume is substantial ($N=85{,}054$), target Hospital~2 comprises 10 WSIs from 9 patients (3 with nodal metastases). Consequently, patient-level pTNM staging evaluations represent an exploratory proof-of-concept case study; formal clinical non-inferiority trials require multi-center prospective cohorts exceeding $N=140$ patients.
\item \textbf{Exploratory Detection Protocol Amendment:} In the MIDOG 2021 detection experiment, four boundary tiles required a post-halt reduction in tissue eligibility from 0.25 to 0.10, classifying detection findings as protocol-amended exploratory.
\item \textbf{Compute Hardware Heterogeneity:} Classification was evaluated on NVIDIA Tesla V100 and RTX 3090 GPUs, whereas online GPU adaptation was profiled on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 container (14.56 GB VRAM).
\item \textbf{Clinical Environment Validation:} While SafeAdapt implements native audit logging interfaces, formal clinical deployment requires integration with live Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS) and Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIMS).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Selective test-time adaptation and fail-closed runtime monitoring provide critical safeguards against catastrophic model collapse under histopathology domain shifts. In multicenter patch classification, selective gating eliminates Tent collapse and reduces harmful adaptation by $>25\times$, while Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA) reverses negative transfer on histochemical outliers ($0.9360$ on Slide~29; $0.9495 \pm 0.0134$ cohort macro-AUROC). In dense mitotic figure detection, the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR) eliminates false silence alarms on benign stroma, achieving positive transfer across the 1,100-patch benchmark ($F_1 = 0.5160 \pm 0.0050$). By unifying pre-adaptation conformal risk control, trust-region parameter projection, and atomic state rollbacks with deterministic per-WSI episodic resets, UTTA-MED establishes a verified fail-closed medical interlock that bounds parameter drift and preserves patient-level diagnostic staging under severe domain shift.

\section{Formal Derivation and Step-by-Step Proof of Theorem 1}
\label{app:theorem1_proof}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Let feature extractor $\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ produce representations satisfying Assumption 1: $\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \|\phi(x)\|_2 \le R_\phi < \infty$. Let the linear classification head be parameterized by weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 1}$ and bias $b \in \mathbb{R}$, yielding logit $z(x) = W^T \phi(x) + b$ with operator norm $\|W\|_2 \le L_W$ (Assumption 2). Under test-time adaptation across stream $t \in [1, T]$, affine parameters $\theta = (\gamma, \beta)$ are updated. For any target input $x$, let $\phi_0(x)$ denote the feature representation under source parameters $\theta_0$, and $\phi_T(x)$ denote the representation under adapted parameters $\theta_T$. By the enforced $L_\infty$ trust region constraint, $\|\theta_T - \theta_0\|_\infty \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}$.

Because BatchNorm normalizes features coordinate-wise, the parameter displacement induces a feature representation perturbation satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\|\phi_T(x) - \phi_0(x)\|_2 \le \|\theta_T - \theta_0\|_\infty \|\phi_0(x)\|_2 \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}} R_\phi.
\end{equation}
Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the linear logit difference yields:
\begin{align}
|z_T(x) - z_0(x)| &= |W^T (\phi_T(x) - \phi_0(x))| \notag \\
&\le \|W\|_2 \|\phi_T(x) - \phi_0(x)\|_2 \le L_W R_\phi \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{align}
The output probability is given by the sigmoid function $p(x) = \sigma(z(x)) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z(x)}}$. By the Mean Value Theorem, there exists $\xi$ between $z_0(x)$ and $z_T(x)$ such that:
\begin{equation}
|\sigma(z_T(x)) - \sigma(z_0(x))| = |\sigma'(\xi)| \cdot |z_T(x) - z_0(x)|.
\end{equation}
Since $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$, the derivative achieves its global maximum at $\sigma(z) = 0.5$, yielding $\sup_{z \in \mathbb{R}} |\sigma'(z)| = \frac{1}{4}$. Substituting the logit perturbation bound into the sigmoid derivative bound gives:
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} |\sigma(z_T(x)) - \sigma(z_0(x))| \le \frac{1}{4} L_W R_\phi \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{equation}
For empirical source weights, $\|W_{\mathrm{head}}\|_2 = 1.8421$, normalized feature ball radius $R_\phi \le 2.0$, and trust region radius $\epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}} = 0.05$. Evaluating the numerical bound yields:
\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{\max} \le \frac{1}{4} (1.8421) (2.0) (0.05) = 0.04605 \approx 0.0460.
\end{equation}
For any target sample whose baseline prediction margin satisfies $|p_0(x) - 0.5| > \Delta p_{\max} = 0.0460$, the output probability cannot cross the classification threshold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $p_0(x) > 0.5460$, then $p_T(x) \ge p_0(x) - \Delta p_{\max} > 0.5000$.
\item If $p_0(x) < 0.4540$, then $p_T(x) \le p_0(x) + \Delta p_{\max} < 0.5000$.
\end{enumerate}
Because samples outside the $\pm 0.0460$ margin cannot cross the decision threshold, global single-class attractor collapse ($p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$) is mathematically precluded for these samples across the full adaptation trajectory.
\end{IEEEproof}
