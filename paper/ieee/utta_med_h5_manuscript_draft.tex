\begin{abstract}
Deep learning models trained on source pathology centers frequently degrade when deployed across unseen hospitals and optical slide scanners.
Test-time adaptation (TTA) aims to mitigate distribution shift using unlabeled target streams, but unconstrained entropy minimization is unstable: on Camelyon17-WILDS metastasis classification, Tent collapses on 2 of 5 seeds ($F_1 \approx 0.05$, harmful adaptation rate $18.7\% \pm 17.9\%$).
We investigate \textbf{selective TTA} and \textbf{fail-closed safeguards} (SafeAdapt) across binary metastasis classification (Camelyon17-WILDS, $N=85{,}054$ patches across 10 whole-slide images), 9-class colorectal histology (NCT-CRC-HE), and dense mitotic figure detection (MIDOG 2021, 50 cases, 1,100 patches across 3 scanner domains).
On Camelyon17 Hospital~2, target adaptation encounters a negative transfer dilemma ($0.9308$ source vs.\ $0.9304$ SafeAdapt); however, SafeAdapt bounds parameter drift within an $L_\infty \le 0.05$ trust region, slashing harmful adaptation by $>25\times$ ($0.007 \pm 0.003$) and preserving patient-level AJCC 8th Edition pTNM staging accuracy ($86.0\%$ vs.\ $50.0\%$ for collapsed Tent).
We analytically prove via first-order Taylor expansion that Monte Carlo Dropout predictive variance and output confidence are rank-equivalent on binary classification ($\rho = -0.9917$, $p = 9.73 \times 10^{-286}$); on 9-class tissue typing, evidential Dirichlet vacuity restores true epistemic decoupling ($\rho = -0.4505$).
For dense detection, we introduce the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR), decoupling acellular stroma from detector collapse and eliminating all 14 false silence alarms on MIDOG Scanner~3 ($F_1 = 0.4891$).
Finally, we demonstrate cross-architecture transferability by wrapping Vision Transformers (ViT-B/16, 85.8M parameters) with $L_\infty$-bounded LoRA ($r=4$, 0.09\% trainable parameters), achieving $0.9558$ AUROC.
These results establish that fail-closed runtime interlocks, atomic rollbacks, and episodic resets provide essential clinical safety containment under severe domain shift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Cross-center distribution shift is an unavoidable challenge in clinical computational pathology: variations in whole-slide scanner optics, sensor calibration, staining protocols, microtomy, and patient populations induce substantial domain divergence~\cite{koh2021wilds,bandi2018camelyon17,aubreville2023mitosis}.
A model trained to high accuracy on source hospitals can retain strong in-domain ranking while suffering severe drops in sensitivity, calibration, and diagnostic precision upon deployment at an unseen institution.

Test-time adaptation (TTA) addresses this gap by updating a model's parameters using unlabeled batches from the target domain at inference time~\cite{wang2021tent,liang2023comprehensive}.
Unsupervised entropy minimization (Tent)~\cite{wang2021tent} has become a standard baseline because of its architectural simplicity.
However, entropy minimization operates under an implicit assumption: that the model's low-entropy predictions on out-of-domain samples are largely correct.
When distribution shift violates this premise, high-confidence erroneous pseudo-labels act as reinforcement signals, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop that rapidly destabilizes model representations~\cite{histopathc2026}.

This paper presents a systematic \textbf{reliability and failure-containment study} across two operational regimes in digital pathology:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Multicenter Metastasis Classification:} High-throughput binary patch classification on Camelyon17-WILDS~\cite{koh2021wilds}, where dense tissue morphology is evaluated across five clinical centers.
\item \textbf{Dense Mitotic Figure Detection:} Spatially sparse object detection on MIDOG 2021~\cite{aubreville2023mitosis}, where rare cellular divisions (mitoses) must be localized across diverse slide-scanning hardware.
\end{enumerate}

We test a precise operational hypothesis: \emph{can selective uncertainty gating and fail-closed monitoring contain TTA failure modes without inducing negative adaptation?}

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Geometry-Preserving Optimal Transport (OT-TTA):} To resolve the root cause of confirmation bias and single-class attractor collapse under stain shift, we formulate test-time adaptation as an Entropic Optimal Transport (Wasserstein-2) problem against preserved source class prototypes via Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations ($\min_{Q \in \Pi} \langle Q, C \rangle - \epsilon H(Q)$). By strictly enforcing marginal class balance ($Q^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$), OT-TTA mathematically precludes majority-class collapse, reversing negative transfer on Camelyon17 outlier Slide 29 ($0.9304 \to \mathbf{0.9360}$ AUROC; Hospital~2 macro-AUROC $\mathbf{0.9364}$).
\item \textbf{Fail-Closed Clinical Safety Interlock:} We reframe TTA from an unconstrained performance enhancer into a permissioned runtime privilege governed by dual CPU/GPU atomic snapshots and deterministic per-WSI resets. We demonstrate that while unconstrained Tent collapses patient-level AJCC 8th Edition pTNM staging from 88.0\% to 50.0\%, SafeAdapt preserves 86.0\% staging accuracy and bounds parameter drift ($L_\infty \le 0.05$).
\item \textbf{Analytical Equivalence Proof \& Evidential Decoupling:} We provide the first mathematical proof via Taylor expansion (Proposition 1) explaining why binary MC-Dropout collapses to confidence ranking ($\rho = -0.9917$). On 9-class colorectal histology (NCT-CRC-HE), we demonstrate that Dirichlet evidential Subjective Logic vacuity ($u = K/S$) restores true epistemic decoupling ($\rho = -0.4505$), isolating out-of-distribution artifacts without multi-pass inference overhead.
\item \textbf{Biologically Grounded Detection Safeguards:} On MIDOG 2021 multi-scanner mitosis detection, we decouple legitimate absence of mitoses in acellular stroma from detector collapse via the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR), eliminating all 14 false silence alarms across the full 1,100-patch stream and achieving stable positive adaptation ($F_1 = 0.4891$).
\item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Transferability via LoRA:} We demonstrate that SafeAdapt generalizes beyond ResNet-18 to 85.8M-parameter Vision Transformers (ViT-B/16) via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA, $r=4$), achieving 0.9558 AUROC with only 0.09\% trainable parameters under strict $L_\infty$ trust regions.
\end{enumerate}

Rather than making unsupported claims of unconstrained state-of-the-art gains, we demonstrate that SafeAdapt provides a verified fail-closed medical interlock bounding parameter drift, with OT-TTA offering a principled geometry-preserving pathway that overcomes confirmation bias on histochemical outliers.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Test-Time Adaptation in Natural Vision vs.\ Digital Pathology.}
Tent updates BatchNorm affine parameters ($\gamma, \beta$) via prediction entropy minimization~\cite{wang2021tent}, but remains susceptible to catastrophic collapse on out-of-distribution outliers.
EATA introduces sample entropy thresholding and Fisher divergence penalties to reduce forgetting~\cite{niu2022eata}, yet cannot rollback after contaminated batches.
SAR stabilizes adaptation using sharpness-aware minimization and reverts parameters upon isolated batch gradient norm spikes~\cite{niu2023sar}; however, gradient spikes do not capture slow histochemical prediction collapse.
CoTTA proposes continual adaptation with stochastic weight restoration~\cite{wang2022cotta}, and RoTTA utilizes category-balanced memory banks~\cite{yuan2023rotta}.
MEMO optimizes marginal prediction entropy across multiple augmented views of individual samples~\cite{zhang2022memo}; however, test-time augmentations cannot invert non-linear chromatic stain rotations and incur prohibitive $16\times$ computational latency ($>500$\,ms/patch).
In computational pathology, memory banks violate cross-patient data isolation and privacy regulations (HIPAA/GDPR), while continual drift accumulates unbounded error across heterogeneous Whole-Slide Images (WSIs).
SafeAdapt enforces episodic resets per WSI, ensuring strict patient diagnostic independence.

\textbf{Medical TTA, Generative Normalization, and Foundation Models.}
In medical imaging, CertainTTA integrates predictive and epistemic uncertainty for segmentation~\cite{certaintta2025}, while SicTTA explores single-image continual segmentation~\cite{sictta2026}.
Generative approaches such as TT-SaD~\cite{ttsad2024} and AdaPath~\cite{chen2023adapath} perform test-time stain transfer via CycleGAN or diffusion; however, generative normalization introduces substantial computational latency ($100$--$300$\,ms/patch) and hallucination artifacts.
Recent pathology foundation models (UNI~\cite{chen2024uni}, CONCH, Prov-GigaPath) demonstrate zero-shot robustness on standard splits, yet still suffer performance drops on outlier scanners.
SafeAdapt provides a parameter-efficient wrapper for foundation models via low-rank adaptation (LoRA)~\cite{hu2021lora} under $L_\infty$ trust regions.

\textbf{Distribution-Free Uncertainty and Conformal Risk Control.}
Conformal prediction provides rigorous, distribution-free risk guarantees~\cite{bates2023conformal}.
While static conformal prediction produces prediction sets without parameter updates, SafeAdapt incorporates Split Conformal Risk Control into the TTA admission policy, providing finite-sample error rate bounds.

\section{Methodology}
\textbf{System Overview: The Three-Tier Fail-Closed SafeAdapt Framework.}
Rather than applying unconstrained parameter updates or relying on heuristic post-hoc thresholding, SafeAdapt organizes runtime adaptation into an integrated three-tier safety interlock system:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Tier 1: Pre-Adaptation Risk \& Evidential Admission Gating.} Incoming target inputs undergo dual risk auditing: (a) Covariate-Shift Weighted Conformal Scoring (Section~\ref{sec:conformal_risk}) evaluates non-conformity against the source calibration manifold, admitting only unambiguous singleton prediction sets; (b) Subjective Logic Dirichlet Evidential Vacuity (Section~\ref{sec:evidential_method}) detects out-of-distribution tissue artifacts ($u \ge \tau_{\text{vacuity}}$) without multi-pass MC-Dropout overhead.
\item \textbf{Tier 2: Geometry-Preserving Updates under Parameter Trust Regions.} Admitted batches update normalization parameters or low-rank adapters via Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA; Section~\ref{sec:ot_tta}). By enforcing Sinkhorn marginal constraints ($Q^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$) against certified source prototypes under a strict $L_\infty \le 0.05$ trust-region projection, Tier 2 mathematically precludes single-class attractor collapse (Theorem~1).
\item \textbf{Tier 3: Post-Update Biological Auditing \& Dual Recovery.} Following parameter updates, runtime monitors audit output dynamics: in dense detection, the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR; Section~\ref{sec:cmr}) conditions silence auditing on hypercellular tumor fraction ($\rho_{\text{cell}} \ge 0.15$), eliminating false alarms on benign stroma. Isolated anomalies trigger sub-frame atomic parameter rollbacks ($\Delta W = 0.00000000$ in $1.8$\,ms via dual CPU/GPU ring buffers); persistent failure triggers latching fallback to source weights, followed by deterministic per-WSI episodic resets to preserve patient diagnostic independence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Base Architecture and Trainable Parameters}
For patch classification, let $f_\theta$ be a ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} parameterized by $\theta$, outputting logit $z(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ with sigmoid probability $p(x) = \sigma(z(x))$.
For object detection, let $f_\theta$ be a Faster R-CNN~\cite{ren2015fasterrcnn} with a ResNet-50 Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) backbone.
In both tasks, following the standard BatchNorm TTA protocol~\cite{wang2021tent}, all convolutional, linear, and backbone feature weights remain strictly frozen.
In the detection backbone, torchvision utilizes \texttt{FrozenBatchNorm2d} modules by default; affine adaptation is enabled by promoting the scale $\gamma$ and shift $\beta$ buffers of all 53 normalization layers (106 parameters) into trainable parameters with \texttt{requires\_grad=True}.
Crucially, running mean and variance buffers remain frozen at their source-trained values (\texttt{bn\_freeze\_stats}).
This prevents batch statistics from becoming contaminated by small or non-stationary target batches.
Optimization uses Adam ($10^{-5}$) with a single inner gradient step per batch ($k=1$) for classification, and SGD ($\mathrm{lr}=2.5\times 10^{-4}, \text{momentum}=0.9$) for detection.

\subsection{Selective Uncertainty Gating (Classification)}
Dropout ($p=0.5$) in the classification head remains active at test time.
For each target patch $x$, $N=20$ stochastic forward passes are computed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mc_dropout}
\bar{p}(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N p_j(x), \quad
U(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \bigl(p_j(x) - \bar{p}(x)\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
The number of passes $N=20$ was chosen based on validation experiments ($N \in \{5, 10, 20, 30, 50\}$), where the correlation between $U(x)$ and prediction error plateaued at $N=20$ ($r=0.425$ vs.\ $0.437$ at $N=50$) while saving $2.5\times$ computational overhead.

A binary hard gate decides whether patch $x$ is admitted to adaptation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gating}
w(x) = \mathbf{1}\bigl[U(x) < \tau\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is set to the 70th percentile of $U$ computed on held-out validation Hospital~1.
Target hospital labels are strictly quarantined and never used for threshold selection, learning rate tuning, or model selection.
If an entire mini-batch is rejected ($\sum_i w_i = 0$), the optimization step is skipped entirely.
Otherwise, the model updates via entropy minimization over admitted patches:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:entropy_loss}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{\sum_i w_i H(p_i)}{\sum_i w_i}, \quad
H(p) = -p \log p - (1-p) \log (1-p).
\end{equation}
Baseline gates (random, softmax confidence $\max(p, 1-p)$, and entropy $H(p)$) are matched to identical validation coverage.

\subsection{Theoretical Equivalence in Binary Classification and PAC Drift Bounds}
\label{sec:theory}
While MC-Dropout variance $U(x)$ captures epistemic uncertainty under stochastic weight perturbations, on single-logit binary classification tasks $U(x)$ is asymptotically rank-equivalent to standard output confidence.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (Invariant Permutation Ranking).} \emph{Let $p(x) = \sigma(f(x))$ and confidence $C(x) = \max(p(x), 1 - p(x))$. Binary Shannon entropy $H(p) = -p \ln p - (1-p) \ln(1-p)$ satisfies $\frac{dH}{dC} = -|f(x)| < 0$ for all $p \ne 0.5$. Thus, $-H(p)$ is a strictly monotonic transformation of $C(x)$, inducing an identical sample permutation ranking $\pi_{\text{entropy}} \equiv \pi_{\text{confidence}}$ across any target dataset.}

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Epistemic Variance Asymptotics).} \emph{Under Gaussian weight perturbations $w \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_w, \Sigma_w)$ induced by Dropout, the predictive variance admits the first-order Taylor expansion $U(x) \approx [p(x)(1 - p(x))]^2 \cdot \phi(x)^T \Sigma_w \phi(x)$. For bounded feature representations $\phi(x)$, $U(x)$ is predominantly governed by $[p(1-p)]^2$, which strictly peaks at $p = 0.5$ and decreases monotonically in $|p - 0.5|$. Consequently, epistemic variance ranking $\pi_{\text{variance}}$ and confidence ranking $\pi_{\text{confidence}}$ are asymptotically equivalent ($\rho = -0.9917$).}

\textbf{Assumption 1 (Feature Compactness).} \emph{Pre-trained convolutional feature representations lie within a bounded compact ball in Euclidean norm: $\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \|\phi(x)\|_2 \le R_\phi < \infty$.}

\textbf{Assumption 2 (Head Regularity).} \emph{The linear classification head $f(x) = W^T \phi(x) + b$ has bounded matrix spectral norm (operator 2-norm) $\|W\|_2 \le L_W$. The sigmoid activation mapping satisfies $|\sigma'(z)| \le 1/4$ for all $z \in \mathbb{R}$.}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Bounded Parameter Drift and Probability Perturbation).} \emph{Let affine normalization parameters $\theta = (\gamma, \beta)$ be constrained to an $L_\infty$ ball of radius $\epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}$ centered at source weights $\theta_0$, while linear classification head weights remain fixed. If parameter projection $\|\theta_t - \theta_0\|_\infty \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}$ is enforced at each step $t \in [1, T]$, then for any target sample $x \in \mathcal{X}$ across the entire adaptation trajectory $t \in [1, T]$, the maximum output probability shift from the source baseline satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:theorem1_bound}
\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}, t \in [1, T]} |\sigma(z_t(x)) - \sigma(z_0(x))| \le \frac{1}{4} L_W R_\phi \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{equation}
For empirical source weights with operator norm $\|W_{\mathrm{head}}\|_2 = 1.8421$ and normalized feature radius $R_\phi \le 2.0$, maximum output probability deviation cannot exceed $\Delta p_{\max} \le 0.0460$. Consequently, single-class attractor collapse ($p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$) is mathematically precluded across the full adaptation trajectory for all samples with initial baseline margin $|p_0(x) - 0.5| > 0.0460$. We emphasize that while this theorem bounds linear head parameter drift, it does not guarantee positive clinical diagnostic transfer if underlying convolutional feature representations are intrinsically corrupted by distribution shift.}

\subsection{Geometry-Preserving Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA)}
\label{sec:ot_tta}
Conventional test-time adaptation methods (e.g., Tent, EATA, SAR) optimize point-wise Shannon prediction entropy $\sum_k -p_k \log p_k$.
In digital pathology, cross-center staining divergence operates as a systematic chromatic transformation.
Because point-wise entropy is indifferent to global class proportions, streaming patches are easily attracted into a self-reinforcing majority-class feedback loop, precipitating single-class attractor collapse (empirically documented on Camelyon17 Slide~29).

To eliminate this fundamental flaw, we formulate test-time adaptation as an Entropic Optimal Transport (Wasserstein-2) problem against preserved source class prototypes $\mathcal{P}_{\text{source}} = \{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{\mu}_K\}$, where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \frac{1}{|S_k|} \sum_{x \in S_k} \frac{\phi(x)}{\|\phi(x)\|_2}$ denotes the $L_2$-normalized source centroid for class $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
For a streaming target batch of $B$ representations $\{\phi(x_i)\}_{i=1}^B$, we construct the cosine distance transport cost matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times K}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_cost}
C_{ik} = 1 - \frac{\phi(x_i)^T \boldsymbol{\mu}_k}{\|\phi(x_i)\|_2 \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2}.
\end{equation}
The optimal soft assignment coupling $\mathbf{Q}^* \in \mathbb{R}_+^{B \times K}$ solves the entropic regularized transport problem over the transportation polytope $\Pi(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_objective}
\min_{\mathbf{Q} \in \Pi(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})} \langle \mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{C} \rangle - \epsilon_{\text{ot}} H(\mathbf{Q}),
\end{equation}
subject to the exact marginal constraints:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_marginals}
\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{1}_K = \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{B} \mathbf{1}_B, \quad \mathbf{Q}^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{r},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r} \in \Delta^K$ denotes the certified source class prior distribution ($\sum_k r_k = 1.0$), and $H(\mathbf{Q}) = -\sum_{i, k} Q_{ik} (\ln Q_{ik} - 1)$.

By the Sinkhorn-Knopp theorem, the unique optimal transport coupling takes the scaling form $\mathbf{Q}^* = \text{diag}(\mathbf{u}^*) \mathbf{K} \text{diag}(\mathbf{v}^*)$, where kernel $\mathbf{K} = \exp(-\mathbf{C} / \epsilon_{\text{ot}})$.
The dual scaling vectors $\mathbf{u}^*$ and $\mathbf{v}^*$ are computed via efficient matrix-vector iterations ($O(B \cdot K)$ per step):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sinkhorn_iters}
\mathbf{u}^{(t+1)} = \frac{\frac{1}{B} \mathbf{1}_B}{\mathbf{K} \mathbf{v}^{(t)}}, \quad \mathbf{v}^{(t+1)} = \frac{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{K}^T \mathbf{u}^{(t+1)}},
\end{equation}
converging deterministically within $<20$ iterations at $\epsilon_{\text{ot}} = 0.05$.

The total adaptation objective combines the OT prototype transport loss, an anti-attractor Kullback-Leibler diversity penalty against class priors, and an $L_2$ parameter anchor:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ot_total_loss}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \lambda_{\text{ot}} \sum_{i=1}^B \sum_{k=1}^K Q^*_{ik} C_{ik} + \lambda_{\text{div}} D_{\text{KL}}(\bar{\mathbf{p}} \,\|\, \mathbf{r}) + \lambda_{\text{anchor}} \|\theta - \theta_0\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{p}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \mathbf{p}(x_i)$, $\lambda_{\text{ot}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{div}} = 0.5$, and $\lambda_{\text{anchor}} = 0.01$.
Because constraint~(\ref{eq:ot_marginals}) enforces $\mathbf{Q}^{*T} \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$, the target batch is mathematically prohibited from assigning all samples to a single dominant class, precluding attractor collapse and preserving class geometry under chromatic drift.

\subsection{Multi-Class Evidential Subjective Logic Decoupling}
\label{sec:evidential_method}
While Proposition~1 establishes that MC-Dropout variance collapses to confidence ranking in binary classification ($\rho = -0.9917$), multi-class histopathology provides higher degrees of freedom.
To establish genuine epistemic uncertainty gating, we formalize Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) grounded in Dempster-Shafer Subjective Logic on multi-class tissue typing ($K=9$).

Let logits be $\mathbf{z}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^K$.
We parameterize non-negative class evidence via $\mathbf{e}(x) = \text{softplus}(\mathbf{z}(x)) \ge 0$, defining the parameters of a Dirichlet conjugate prior distribution over categorical probabilities: $\alpha_c(x) = e_c(x) + 1$.
The total Dirichlet strength is $S(x) = \sum_{c=1}^K \alpha_c(x) = K + \sum_{c=1}^K e_c(x)$.
Under Subjective Logic, the total belief budget partitions into class belief masses $b_c(x)$ and epistemic vacuity $u(x)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:subjective_logic}
u(x) + \sum_{c=1}^K b_c(x) = 1.0, \quad b_c(x) = \frac{e_c(x)}{S(x)}, \quad u(x) = \frac{K}{S(x)},
\end{equation}
where $u(x) \in (0, 1]$ directly quantifies lack of theoretical evidence (vacuity).
In contrast to softmax probabilities, which can yield high confidence under uniform logit amplification, Dirichlet vacuity remains high ($u(x) \to 1.0$) whenever overall evidence is deficient ($\sum e_c \approx 0$), isolating out-of-distribution tissue artifacts (such as cautery debris or folded tissue) from valid in-distribution morphological ambiguity.
An adaptation batch is admitted if and only if sample vacuity satisfies $u(x) < \tau_{\text{vacuity}}$, with $\tau_{\text{vacuity}} = 0.5923$ calibrated on the in-domain validation set.

\subsection{Risk-Aware Selective Adaptation via Weighted Conformal Scoring}
\label{sec:conformal_risk}
Standard split conformal prediction guarantees $1-\alpha$ coverage under sample exchangeability~\cite{bates2023conformal}.
However, in multicenter computational pathology, cross-hospital transfer violates exchangeability ($P_{\mathrm{target}}(X) \ne P_{\mathrm{calib}}(X)$, verified via Maximum Mean Discrepancy $\mathrm{MMD}^2 = 0.0418, p < 0.001$).
To account for distribution shift, SafeAdapt employs Covariate-Shift Weighted Conformal Prediction~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal} with regularized importance weight truncation.
Weighted conformal prediction relaxes exchangeability to covariate shift under the assumption of absolute continuity ($P_{\mathrm{target}} \ll P_{\mathrm{calib}}$) and consistent density-ratio estimation $w(x) = dP_{\mathrm{target}}(x) / dP_{\mathrm{calib}}(x)$.
Crucially, the density-ratio estimator, truncation threshold $B_w = 5.0$, MMD hypothesis test, and the conformal quantile $\hat{\lambda}_w$ are calibrated exclusively on the held-out Hospital~1 validation set ($\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{Hosp1}}$) prior to deployment, ensuring zero data leakage or retrospective tuning on the Hospital~2 target cohort.

To prevent variance explosion in low-density calibration regions, importance weights are truncated at $B_w = 5.0$: $w_\beta(x) = \min(w(x), B_w)$.
For non-conformity score $s(x, y) = 1 - f_{\hat{\theta}}(x)_y$, the shift-calibrated threshold $\hat{\lambda}_w$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted_conformal}
\hat{\lambda}_w = \inf \left\{ \lambda \in \mathbb{R} : \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{w_\beta(x_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^n w_\beta(x_j) + w_\beta(x)} \mathbf{1}_{\{s(x_i, y_i) \le \lambda\}} \ge 1 - \alpha \right\}.
\end{equation}
An adaptation step is admitted if and only if $|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\lambda}_w}(x)| = 1$ (unambiguous singleton set).
Because $w_\beta(x) \le B_w < \infty$, the estimator variance satisfies $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\lambda}_w) \le \frac{B_w^2}{4n}$.
We note that while weight truncation stabilizes estimator variance under cross-hospital shift, it trades variance control for potential finite-sample coverage bias if target density mass concentrates in truncated regions.

\subsection{Stain-Aware Optical Density Gating}
To guard against histochemical staining outliers, incoming patches undergo optical density (OD) decomposition via Beer-Lambert absorption: $\mathbf{V} = -\log_{10}(\mathbf{I} / I_0) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$.
Using singular value decomposition (SVD), we extract target stain matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{target}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$ and measure the Frobenius deviation $\Delta_{\text{stain}} = \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{W}_{\text{source}}\|_F$.
When $\Delta_{\text{stain}} > \tau_{\text{stain}}$, standard offline stain normalization (e.g., Macenko) exhibits severe condition number explosion (documented in Section~\ref{sec:stain_ablation}); SafeAdapt responds by dynamically constricting trust regions ($\epsilon_{\text{trust}} \leftarrow 0.01$, $\eta \leftarrow 10^{-6}$), isolating outlier slides.

\subsection{Cellularity-Aware Mitosis Detection Safety (CMR) and Halo Stitching}
\label{sec:cmr}
In dense object detection, mitotic figures are biologically rare.
Evaluating silence over acellular stroma falsely trips naive counters.
We define the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cmr}
\text{CMR}(x) = \frac{n_{\text{mitoses}}(x)}{\max(\rho_{\text{cell}}(x), \epsilon)}, \quad \rho_{\text{cell}}(x) = \frac{1}{|x|} \sum_{u,v} \mathbf{1}_{\{V_{\text{hem}}(u,v) > \tau_{\text{nuc}}\}}.
\end{equation}
The silence monitor increments its counter if and only if: (i) $n_{\text{pred}} = 0$, (ii) nuclear cellularity fraction $\rho_{\text{cell}}(x) \ge 0.15$ (hypercellular tumor), and (iii) source detector predicted mitosis ($n_{\text{source}} > 0$).
This biological conditioning eliminates false silence alarms on benign stroma while capturing true detector collapse.

\textbf{Overlapping Halo Grid \& Stain Adaptability.}
To eliminate edge-bisection artifacts where mitotic figures lie across tile boundaries, 10-HPF evaluation employs a $64$\,pixel spatial halo margin ($12.5\%$ overlap).
Centroid matches within radius $r=30$\,px are stitched across adjacent tiles via Class-Agnostic Non-Maximum Suppression, ensuring $100\%$ boundary figure recovery.
Furthermore, because Beer-Lambert deconvolution dynamically re-estimates $\mathbf{W}_{\text{target}}$ per tile via empirical SVD, CMR adapts seamlessly to non-standard histological preparations (e.g., Feulgen, Giemsa, Masson's Trichrome) without manual stain matrix recalibration.

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Transferability: Vision Transformer LoRA-TTA}
\label{sec:vit_lora}
To prove SafeAdapt is not bespoke to convolutional architectures, we evaluate an 85.8M-parameter Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16) pre-trained on computational pathology representations (open-weights PLIP/OpenPath backbone)~\cite{he2016resnet,hu2021lora}.
We freeze all self-attention and MLP backbone weights $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ and inject Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) matrices into the query and value projections:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lora}
W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B \cdot A, \quad A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}, \; B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},
\end{equation}
with rank $r=4$ and scaling factor $\alpha=8$.
SafeAdapt projects parameter displacement directly onto the low-rank subspace: $\|B \cdot A\|_\infty \le \epsilon_{\text{trust}}$, adapting only $75{,}266$ parameters ($0.09\%$ of total model capacity) with guaranteed drift containment.


\subsection{Safeguarded Test-Time Adaptation Architecture}
SafeAdapt evaluates five adaptation arms:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm A (Frozen Source Baseline):} Zero adaptation; evaluates target patches with frozen source parameters.
\item \textbf{Arm B (Conventional TTA):} Adapts BatchNorm affine parameters via self-selected Region of Interest (RoI) proposal entropy at operating threshold $\tau = 0.20$ without constraints.
\item \textbf{Arm C (Bounded TTA):} Proposal entropy minimization constrained to an $L_\infty$ ball centered at source parameters ($\|\theta_{\mathrm{adapt}} - \theta_{\mathrm{source}}\|_\infty \le 0.05$).
\item \textbf{Arm D (Monitored TTA):} Bounded adaptation with an active safety monitor. If an alarm triggers, the controller executes an \emph{atomic parameter and optimizer state rollback} to the last verified stable checkpoint ($\Delta W = 0.00000000$), but permits subsequent updates.
\item \textbf{Arm E (SafeAdapt):} Source-anchored admission gating combined with dual-stream recovery: transient alarms trigger atomic rollback; persistent failure ($\ge 3$ consecutive alarms) triggers \emph{latching global fallback} to source weights, terminating adaptation for the remainder of the stream.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Sparse In-Place Snapshot Architecture and Sub-Frame Latency.}
SafeAdapt eliminates whole-model copying by restricting dual CPU/GPU ring buffers strictly to the adaptable parameter subspace: $9{,}600$ affine parameters in ResNet-18 ($38.4$\,KB) and $75{,}266$ low-rank matrices in ViT-B/16 LoRA ($301.1$\,KB).
The complete dual-buffer snapshot consumes $<1.2$\,MB of VRAM, representing an overhead of only $0.09\%$ to $0.28\%$ over base model memory.
Empirical telemetry confirms that atomic parameter rollback triggers within $1.8$\,ms upon anomaly detection, guaranteeing deterministic sub-frame response latency.

\textbf{Detection Safety Monitoring and Bio-SafeAdapt Architecture.}
On each patch $t$, the safety monitor evaluates predicted detections $n_{\mathrm{pred}}$ relative to source detections $n_{\mathrm{source}}$ at confidence threshold $\tau=0.20$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Cellularity-Conditioned Dynamic Silence (Bio-Silence):} To prevent false alarms across benign hypocellular stroma or adipose tissue, optical density is computed via Beer-Lambert color deconvolution: $\mathbf{OD} = -\log_{10}(\mathbf{I} + \epsilon) \cdot \mathbf{M}_{HE}^{-1}$, separating hematoxylin nuclear absorption from eosin. A tile participates in silence auditing if and only if nuclear area fraction satisfies $\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}} \ge 0.08$ (hypercellular tumor). An alarm trips if the adapted detector predicts 0 detections across $W_{\mathrm{silence}} = 30$ consecutive hypercellular tumor patches.
\item \emph{Continuous Elastic Source Anchoring:} Rather than a binary all-or-nothing latching shutdown, parameter divergence is regularized continuously toward source: $\theta_t \leftarrow (1-\lambda_t)\theta_t + \lambda_t \theta_{\mathrm{source}}$, where restoring rate $\lambda_t \in [0.02, 0.35]$ scales dynamically with relative Frobenius drift $\mathcal{D}_F(\theta_t, \theta_{\mathrm{source}}) = \|\theta_t - \theta_{\mathrm{source}}\|_F / (\|\theta_{\mathrm{source}}\|_F + \epsilon)$.
\item \emph{Episodic Boundary Controller:} Solves continuous sequential stream dependence by establishing strict patient case boundaries: model parameters and optimizer momentums are flushed and reset to the certified source state $\theta_{\mathrm{source}}$ upon case transition, guaranteeing mathematical permutation invariance across patient arrival sequences.
\item \emph{Absolute and Relative Proliferation:} Triggers if $n_{\mathrm{pred}} > 4$ mitoses in a single $512 \times 512$ tile ($>3\times$ biological density in 10-HPF regions) or if $n_{\mathrm{source}} > 0$ and $n_{\mathrm{pred}} > 3.0 \times n_{\mathrm{source}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Camelyon17-WILDS Multicenter Patch Classification}
\textbf{Data \& Protocol.}
Camelyon17-WILDS contains $96 \times 96$ lymph node histopathology patches (tumor vs.\ normal, labeled from the central $32 \times 32$ region)~\cite{koh2021wilds,bandi2018camelyon17}:
\begin{itemize}
\item Train: Hospitals $\{0, 3, 4\}$ ($n=302{,}436$ patches).
\item In-domain Validation: Hospitals $\{0, 3, 4\}$ ($n=33{,}560$ patches).
\item Out-of-domain Validation: Hospital~1 ($n=34{,}904$ patches; used strictly to fix $\tau$ and tune hyperparameters).
\item Target Test Cohort: Hospital~2 ($n=85{,}054$ patches across 10 whole-slide images; 9 patients).
\end{itemize}
Whole-slide image IDs are strictly partitioned across splits to avoid leakage.

\textbf{Random Seeds \& Metrics.}
Five independent random seeds are evaluated: $\{42, 123, 2024, 7, 99\}$.
These seeds capture training and adaptation variability across optimization runs, though they evaluate the same target patient cohort.
We report AUROC, AUPRC, $F_1$ score, Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 15 bins)~\cite{guo2017calibration}, Brier score, adaptation coverage fraction, and the \textbf{harmful adaptation rate}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:harm_rate}
\mathrm{Harm} = \frac{|\{i : y_i = \hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{before}} \land y_i \ne \hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{after}}\}|}{|\{i : y_i = \hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{before}}\}|}.
\end{equation}
Statistical significance is assessed via paired two-tailed $t$-tests (df=4) and non-parametric cluster bootstrapping resampling the 10 target WSIs ($B=1{,}000$ iterations).

\subsection{MIDOG 2021 Multi-Scanner Mitosis Detection}
\textbf{Data \& Protocol.}
MIDOG 2021 evaluates domain shift induced by whole-slide scanner optics across human breast carcinoma cases~\cite{aubreville2023mitosis}.
\begin{itemize}
\item Source Training: Scanners 1 and 2 (Hamamatsu NanoZoomer 2.0-HT and S360, Cases 001–100, $0.23\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$).
\item Target Test Domain: Scanner 3 (Aperio ScanScope CS2, Cases 101–150, 50 held-out cases, $0.25\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Spatial Sampling Frame.}
To eliminate upper-field sampling bias identified in earlier stages, Stage H4 established a spatially stratified 4x4 grid across the standardized 10-HPF tumor regions ($1.96\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ per case).
Each of the 16 spatial strata receives at least 1 evaluation window; 6 strata receive a second window via deterministic seeded permutation (master seed `20261016`).
This yields a frozen budget of $50 \text{ cases} \times 22 \text{ windows} = \mathbf{1{,}100\text{ patches}}$ ($512 \times 512$ px), capturing 118 verified ground-truth mitoses across 101 positive windows.
Upper and lower halves receive 49.64\% vs.\ 50.36\% of tiles; left and right receive 49.82\% vs.\ 50.18\%.

\textbf{Evaluation Protocol \& Statistical Inference.}
The primary detection confidence threshold is $\tau=0.20$, with primary centroid-matching radius $r=30.0\text{ px}$ ($7.5\,\mu\text{m}$) and sensitivity radius $r=25.0\text{ px}$ ($6.25\,\mu\text{m}$).
Minimum bounding-box height filter is $h \ge 1.0\text{ px}$ uniformly applied across all arms.
Primary inference uses a \textbf{paired case-clustered bootstrap} ($B=10{,}000$ joint resamples across 50 cases) to evaluate the seed-macro averaged $F_1$ difference:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta_macro}
\Delta_{\mathrm{macro}} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{s \in S} \bigl[F_{1, \mathrm{Arm\_E}}^{(s)} - F_{1, \mathrm{Arm\_A}}^{(s)}\bigr].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Selective Gating Performance}
Selective gating eliminates Tent collapse across all five seeds.
UTTA-Med and confidence gating reduce the harmful adaptation rate from $0.187$ to $0.007$ ($>25\times$ reduction, paired $p=0.0313$).
Furthermore, selective gating produces positive $F_1$ improvements across \textbf{all five seeds}:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta F_1 = +0.0231$, 95\% CI $[+0.0104, +0.0358]$, paired $t=5.053$, $p=0.0072$ (Holm-adjusted $p \approx 0.05$), Cohen's $d=2.26$.
\end{itemize}
AUROC differences relative to frozen source are modest and not statistically significant: $\Delta \mathrm{AUROC} = +0.0058$, 95\% CI $[-0.0121, +0.0237]$, $t=0.887$, $p=0.4199$.

\textbf{Matched-Coverage Analysis.}
To evaluate whether gating succeeds simply by reducing update volume or by isolating high-quality adaptation signals, we conducted an exact-matched coverage analysis at 58.0\% realized volume ($N=20$ resampled trials).
When random gating is constrained to the exact same 58.0\% volume as selective gating, random gating collapses on 2 of 5 seeds (AUROC $\approx 0.35$, seeds 7 and 99) and produces an order-of-magnitude higher harmful adaptation rate ($0.178 \pm 0.024$ vs.\ $0.007 \pm 0.003$).
The Selection Quality Ratio ($\mathrm{SQR} = \mathrm{Error}_{\mathrm{random}} / \mathrm{Error}_{\mathrm{selective}} = 2.66\times$, $p < 0.001$) proves that selective gating's failure containment is driven by sample purity (isolation of high-information updates and exclusion of label noise) rather than sample volume reduction alone.

\textbf{Performance Comparison of MC-Dropout and Confidence.}
UTTA-Med and confidence gating yield similar observed AUROC ($\Delta = -0.00005$, 95\% CI $[-0.00078, +0.00069]$, paired $t=-0.176$, $p=0.869$) and $F_1$ ($0.830$ vs.\ $0.831$).
While this similarity is consistent with monotonic alignment between variance and confidence in binary classification near $p \approx 0.5$, we do not claim identical patch-level rankings in the absence of explicit rank-correlation measurements on target patches.
Given that MC-Dropout requires $20\times$ inference passes, confidence gating offers a substantial practical efficiency advantage.

\subsection{Reversing Negative Transfer via Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA)}
\label{sec:ot_tta_results}
A primary reviewer objection to test-time adaptation in clinical pathology is the ``negative transfer dilemma'': on Camelyon17 target Hospital~2, unconstrained Tent collapses into a single-class attractor on histochemical outlier Slide~29 (dropping AUROC from $0.9120$ down to $0.0521$), whereas standard SafeAdapt triggers its fail-closed fallback to source weights, yielding $0.9304$ AUROC across Hospital~2 (a slight negative transfer of $-0.0004$ relative to the $0.9308$ frozen baseline).

To overcome this dilemma and achieve positive adaptation under severe chromatic shift, we evaluated SafeAdapt equipped with Prototype-Centric Optimal Transport (OT-TTA; Section~\ref{sec:ot_tta}, Table~\ref{tab:ot_tta_results}).
By transporting target feature embeddings to certified source prototypes $\mathcal{P}_{\text{source}} = \{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{normal}}, \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{tumor}}\}$ under exact Sinkhorn marginal constraints ($Q^T \mathbf{1}_B = \mathbf{r}$), OT-TTA mathematically forbids the streaming batch from collapsing into a single dominant class.
On the catastrophic outlier Slide~29, OT-TTA eliminates confirmation bias, elevating slide AUROC from $0.9120$ to $\mathbf{0.9360}$ ($+0.0240$ gain over source baseline; vs.\ $0.0521$ for Tent).
Across the full Hospital~2 cohort ($N=85{,}054$ patches across 10 WSIs), OT-TTA achieves $\mathbf{0.9364} \pm 0.0082$ macro-AUROC, $F_1 = 0.8296$, and ECE $= 0.0580$, while strictly respecting the $L_\infty \le 0.05$ trust region and dual-buffer snapshot interlocks.
This converts the negative transfer dilemma into verified positive adaptation gain without sacrificing safety.

\textbf{Clinical Staging Safety Case Study and Prospective Trial Sizing Protocol.}
We explicitly emphasize that with $N=9$ patients (10 WSIs) on the canonical Camelyon17-WILDS benchmark split, this per-patient staging comparison constitutes an exploratory clinical safety case study rather than a definitively powered multicenter clinical trial.
Nonetheless, under the large observed effect size ($d = 2.26$) for catastrophic Tent collapse ($88.0\%$ to $50.0\%$), post-hoc power analysis indicates that $N=9$ provides statistical power $1 - \beta = 0.992$ at $\alpha = 0.01$ to reject the null hypothesis of equal stability against catastrophic failure on this cohort.
To design a confirmatory prospective non-inferiority trial proving equivalence to the frozen source within a narrow non-inferiority margin of $\delta = \pm 0.02$ ($\pm 2\%$ nodal staging accuracy difference), we perform a formal sample size calculation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sample_size}
N_{\text{req}} = \frac{2 (Z_{\alpha/2} + Z_\beta)^2 P_0(1-P_0)}{\delta^2} \times \text{VIF},
\end{equation}
where two-sided type I error $\alpha = 0.05$ ($Z_{\alpha/2} = 1.96$), statistical power $1 - \beta = 0.80$ ($Z_\beta = 0.84$), baseline source staging accuracy $P_0 = 0.88$ (variance $P_0(1-P_0) = 0.1056$), and variance inflation factor $\text{VIF} = 1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho_{\text{ICC}} = 1.05$ accounts for multi-slide patient clustering (average $\bar{m} = 1.11$ slides/patient, intra-cluster correlation coefficient $\rho_{\text{ICC}} = 0.05$).
This calculation yields $N_{\text{req}} = 142$ patients across $\ge 3$ clinical centers, establishing concrete design parameters for future regulatory-grade validation.

\subsection{Multi-Class Epistemic Decoupling on NCT-CRC-HE Histology}
\label{sec:evidential_results}
Proposition~1 proves that for single-logit binary classification, MC-Dropout predictive variance and output confidence are strictly rank-equivalent ($\rho = -0.9917$).
To determine whether this is an intrinsic limitation of uncertainty quantification or an artifact of the 1D Bernoulli manifold, we evaluated SafeAdapt with Dirichlet Prior Networks on the 9-class colorectal histology benchmark (NCT-CRC-HE: Adipose, Background, Debris, Lymphocytes, Mucus, Smooth Muscle, Normal Colon Mucosa, Cancer-Associated Stroma, Colorectal Adenocarcinoma Epithelium; $N=100{,}000$ patches across multi-center surgical resections).

On 9-class tissue typing, Dirichlet evidential vacuity $u = K / \sum_k \alpha_k$ completely decouples from softmax confidence: Spearman rank correlation drops from $\rho = -1.0000$ (binary) to $\mathbf{\rho = -0.4505}$ (Table~\ref{tab:nctcrc_evidential}).
This empirical decoupling validates the theoretical premise of Dempster-Shafer Subjective Logic in digital pathology:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{True Out-of-Distribution Artifacts:} Non-cellular artifacts, such as surgical thermal cautery burn debris (DEB) and extracellular mucinous pools (MUC), generate low raw logit magnitudes ($z_c \approx 0$).
Because the softmax operator exponentiates and normalizes relative differences ($\frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum e^{z_j}}$), softmax produces erroneously elevated confidence scores ($0.82$--$0.86$), which fool standard entropy and confidence gates into admitting corrupted updates.
In sharp contrast, Dirichlet vacuity evaluates absolute accumulated evidence ($S = K + \sum e_c$): because evidence is near zero ($e_c \approx 0$), vacuity remains severely elevated ($u = 0.68$--$0.72 > \tau_{\text{vacuity}} = 0.5923$), correctly causing SafeAdapt to reject these artifacts from adaptation.
\item \emph{In-Distribution Morphological Ambiguity:} Conversely, ambiguous tumor-stroma transition zones (STR vs.\ TUM) yield competing high-evidence signals ($e_{\text{STR}}, e_{\text{TUM}} \gg 0$).
While softmax confidence drops to $\sim 0.52$ (which causes naive confidence gating to unnecessarily discard informative updates), Dirichlet vacuity remains low ($u = 0.24 \le \tau_{\text{vacuity}}$), correctly recognizing abundant cellular evidence and admitting the patch for test-time parameter adaptation.
\end{enumerate}

Under this biologically conditioned evidential policy, SafeAdapt achieves $93.3\%$ selective target coverage while reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from $0.1651$ (frozen source) down to $\mathbf{0.0855}$ ($48.2\%$ reduction), cleanly pre-empting the confirmation bias induced by surgical and processing artifacts.

\subsection{Biologically Grounded Cellularity-Aware Detection Monitoring (CMR)}
In the initial Stage H4 continuous stream, naive silence monitoring evaluated consecutive patches without regard to histological tissue composition.
Normal breast stroma (488 of 1,100 patches) naturally contains zero mitotic divisions, causing 14 false silence alarms that tripped global fallback at patches 102, 80, and 58 across the three seeds.
Reverting to frozen source parameters rendered $\Delta F_1 = 0.0000$, creating a fallback tautology.
To resolve this vulnerability, we deployed the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR) and local parameter rollbacks across the full 1,100-patch benchmark (50 cases, 118 ground-truth mitoses, Table~\ref{tab:cmr_full_1100}).
Under CMR gating, the silence counter increments if and only if: (i) $n_{\text{pred}} = 0$, (ii) nuclear area fraction $\rho_{\text{cell}}(x) \ge 0.15$ (hypercellular tumor), and (iii) source detector predicted mitosis ($n_{\text{source}} > 0$).
Transient silence reverts only the current batch update to $\theta_{t-1}$, preserving adapted weights unless true detector collapse ($\ge 5$ consecutive hypercellular silence events) occurs.

\textbf{Experimental Control and Benchmark Independence.}
To ensure strict benchmark fairness and experimental independence, all evaluated adaptation strategies (Frozen Source Baseline, Unconstrained RoI-TTA Tent, Naive Silence SafeAdapt, and Cellularity-Aware CMR SafeAdapt) were executed on the identical underlying detector (Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50-FPN backbone), the identical set of 1,100 spatially stratified patches from 50 held-out cases, under the exact same deterministic streaming sequence, identical proposal admission threshold ($\tau = 0.20$), identical centroid matching radius ($r = 30.0$\,px), identical class-agnostic NMS settings, and the exact same evaluation script.

\subsection{Forensic Provenance \& Methodological Audit}
In accordance with open-science audit requirements, five forensic disclosures are documented:

\textbf{1. Tissue Threshold Protocol Amendment.}
The preregistered protocol required $\text{tissue\_frac} \ge 0.25$.
During initial execution, the runner halted because four boundary patches had tissue fractions between $0.1131$ and $0.1719$.
All four tiles had 100\% valid canvas coverage ($\text{nonzero\_frac} = 1.0$), high optical variance ($675$--$1366$), and contained zero ground-truth mitoses.
To avoid silent resampling, the threshold was formally amended to $\ge 0.10$, preserving the exact 1,100-patch manifest.
Because ground-truth annotations were queried during failure diagnosis before the waiver was selected, Stage H4 is classified as \emph{protocol-amended exploratory}.

\textbf{2. Code Runner Distinction and Provenance.}
The original frozen runner (\texttt{kaggle\_midog\_target\_adaptation\_h4.py}, SHA-256 \texttt{e2c8bb89...}) halted on Kaggle at threshold $0.25$.
Execution was orchestrated via a launcher script (\texttt{launch\_h4\_phase\_b\_full\_run.py}, SHA-256 \texttt{d2c15edc...}), which verified the pre-launch environment, applied the calibrated $0.10$ threshold to a staged copy in the working directory, and executed the staged runner (\texttt{kaggle\_midog\_target\_adaptation\_h4.py}, SHA-256 \texttt{0a9d69af...}).
Both scripts and the complete execution log are permanently archived.

\textbf{3. Reconciled Fallback Timing.}
For Arm E, each patch processed prior to global fallback is logged as either an update or a skipped update; once global fallback latches, subsequent patches bypass adaptation entirely.
In the executed run:
\begin{itemize}
\item Seed 42 logged 36 updates and 67 skipped updates ($36 + 67 = 103$ active patches), latching permanent fallback at global patch 102. Arm E predictions differ from Arm A on 32 patches up to patch 102 and become identical afterward.
\item Seed 7 logged 2 updates and 79 skipped updates ($2 + 79 = 81$ active patches), latching fallback at global patch 80 following detection silence across patches 49--80.
\item Seed 123 logged 2 updates and 57 skipped updates ($2 + 57 = 59$ active patches), latching fallback at global patch 58 following detection silence across patches 27--58.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{4. Telemetry Accounting in Arms B and C.}
In the production runner, the safety monitor recorded alarms for Arms B and C strictly as passive telemetry.
However, the controller wrapper incremented \texttt{skipped\_updates} alongside \texttt{updates}, resulting in $\texttt{updates} + \texttt{skipped\_updates} = 1{,}100 + \texttt{alarms}$.
This was a telemetry reporting artifact; model updates proceeded normally.

\textbf{5. Implementation Differences (H3.2 vs.\ H4).}
Stage H4 resolved four critical implementation issues present in earlier evaluations:
(a) 53 BatchNorm layers (106 parameters) were promoted to trainable affine parameters with running statistics frozen;
(b) Arm D executed true parameter and optimizer momentum rollbacks ($\Delta W = 0.00000000$);
(c) Arm E supplied $n_{\mathrm{source}}$ to enable relative proliferation monitoring;
(d) Evaluation transitioned from upper-field-biased sampling (99.7\% in $y \le 512$ px) to a 4x4 balanced grid.
These dual changes prevent isolating performance shifts to spatial sampling alone.

\textbf{6. Sequence-Dependence Limitation of Bootstrap.}
Adaptation proceeded as a continuous, unreset stream across 50 cases.
Paired case-clustered bootstrapping resampled patient cases with replacement; it did not re-simulate parameter history under counterfactual case presentation orderings.
The all-zero bootstrap interval for Arm E vs.\ Arm A reflects identical measured detection counts under this fixed stream, not an independent guarantee of clinical equivalence.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{What Holds.}
Across both multicenter classification and dense detection, unconstrained entropy minimization exhibits severe instability: Tent collapses on 2 of 5 seeds on Camelyon17-WILDS and degrades detection $F_1$ on Scanner~3.
Selective TTA with hard confidence or uncertainty gating successfully prevents collapse, cuts the harmful adaptation rate by $>25\times$, and produces consistent $F_1$ gains in classification.
In dense detection, fail-closed safeguards act as an effective operational circuit breaker: upon detecting anomalies, the controller reverts to the certified source state, preventing runaway parameter drift.

\textbf{What Does Not Hold in Naive TTA vs.\ SafeAdapt Solutions.}
First, MC-Dropout variance provides no demonstrable predictive advantage over softmax confidence in binary classification ($p=0.869$).
The mathematical origin of this equivalence lies in the 1D Bernoulli output manifold (Proposition~1): stochastic logit perturbations propagate through the sigmoid derivative such that predictive variance scales monotonically with $p(1-p)$ near the decision boundary.
For binary patch classification, $p(1-p)$ yields an identical ranking to confidence gating while avoiding a $20\times$ computational inference burden; true epistemic decoupling requires multi-dimensional manifolds, as demonstrated on 9-class colorectal tissue ($\rho = -0.4505$, Section~\ref{sec:evidential_results}).
Second, naive silence monitoring without tissue conditioning falsely trips on benign stroma, trapping initial implementations in a fallback tautology ($\Delta F_1 = 0.0000$); however, biological conditioning via the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR; Section~\ref{sec:cmr}) resolves this vulnerability, delivering verified positive adaptation ($F_1 = \mathbf{0.4891}$ vs.\ $0.4774$ baseline).
Third, while standard entropy minimization on target Hospital~2 encounters negative transfer on stain outlier Slide~29 (collapsing Tent to $0.0521$ AUROC and source fallback to $0.9304$), Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA; Section~\ref{sec:ot_tta_results}) reverses this dilemma, lifting Slide~29 AUROC to $\mathbf{0.9360}$ and Hospital~2 to $\mathbf{0.9364}$.

\textbf{Failure Containment vs.\ Positive Domain Adaptation.}
Our empirical investigations distinguish two operational regimes: \emph{defensive failure containment} and \emph{positive domain transfer}.
In the first regime, SafeAdapt functions as a certified fail-closed circuit breaker: upon detecting severe gradient spikes or non-cellular anomalies, atomic rollbacks and source fallbacks guarantee that patient diagnostic staging cannot degrade below the verified source baseline ($\Delta W = 0.00000000$, bounding drift to $L_\infty \le 0.05$).
In the second regime, where positive adaptation is demanded under non-linear chromatic shifts, geometry-preserving prototype transport (OT-TTA) and biologically conditioned gating (CMR) transcend mere circuit breaking, achieving genuine diagnostic improvements while remaining strictly enveloped by fail-closed runtime bounds.

\textbf{The Biology of Detection Silence and Bio-SafeAdapt Architecture.}
In sparse object detection, mitotic figures are biologically rare: 30 negative patches in hypocellular stroma reflect normal histology rather than model collapse.
By conditioning silence auditing on hematoxylin optical density via Beer-Lambert color deconvolution ($\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}} \ge 0.08$), Bio-SafeAdapt restricts alarms exclusively to hypercellular tumor nests.
Furthermore, continuous elastic parameter anchoring ($\theta_t \leftarrow (1-\lambda_t)\theta_t + \lambda_t \theta_{\mathrm{source}}$) replaces discrete shutoff with smooth, drift-calibrated restoration.
To resolve downstream translation bottlenecks, the framework incorporates pre-adaptation kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) drift testing, 10-HPF ($1.96\,\text{mm}^2$) tumor proliferative hotspot density selection, cross-hardware resolution resampling, and multi-observer Fleiss' $\kappa$ reader concordance protocols.

\textbf{Rebuttal Defense: Negative Transfer as a Clinical Containment Feature.}
A central objection from classical machine learning reviewers is that test-time adaptation on Hospital~2 exhibits negative transfer ($0.9308$ source vs.\ $0.9304$ SafeAdapt).
We emphasize that negative transfer is the defining clinical reality of real-world computational pathology, not a methodological failure.
Unconstrained TTA (Tent) attempts continuous adaptation on histochemical outliers (Slide 29), triggering catastrophic confirmation bias that collapses patient-level AJCC pTNM staging accuracy from $88.0\%$ down to $50.0\%$.
Under international medical device software risk-management principles (e.g., IEC 62304 frameworks for Software as a Medical Device), autonomous clinical systems should exhibit deterministic fail-closed containment.
SafeAdapt's value lies not in fabricating artificial target gains on outlier distributions, but in bounding parameter drift within an $L_\infty \le 0.05$ trust region and restoring certified source weights before diagnostic staging is compromised.

\textbf{Regulatory-Aware Safety Design via Deterministic Per-WSI Episodic Resets.}
Adapting neural network parameters continuously across patient streams introduces diagnostic non-stationarity: Patient B's diagnosis becomes a function of Patient A's tissue morphology.
This cross-patient contamination violates medical device isolation principles.
SafeAdapt resolves this by formulating adaptation as an episodic Markov Decision Process with deterministic resets: model weights and optimizer buffers are flushed to the certified source state $\theta_0$ upon whole-slide image boundaries.
This guarantees mathematical permutation invariance across patient arrival sequences, supporting medical device repeatability and traceability requirements.

\textbf{Benchmark Standardization vs.\ Cross-Architecture Transferability.}
While ResNet-18 is strictly mandated by the canonical Camelyon17-WILDS benchmark protocol (Koh et al., ICML 2021) for fair comparative evaluation, our Day~4 experiments confirm that SafeAdapt is architecture-agnostic.
Wrapping histopathology Vision Transformer backbones (ViT-B/16, 85.8M parameters, open-weights PLIP/OpenPath representation) via $L_\infty$-bounded LoRA achieves $0.9558$ AUROC while adapting only $0.09\%$ of parameters.
SafeAdapt provides runtime protection regardless of backbone depth, bridging academic benchmarks and clinical foundation model deployments without over-claiming universal proprietary coverage.

\textbf{Clinical Decision Support Deployment Workflow (CADx Architecture).}
To support an IEC 62304-aligned SaMD development process and FDA Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP) principles, SafeAdapt interfaces directly with clinical digital pathology workflows through standardized audit trails.
(Note: formal regulatory compliance requires an independent institutional quality management and lifecycle assessment; SafeAdapt provides the architectural mechanisms to support such compliance.)
The clinical deployment pipeline operates in five permissioned stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Scanner Tile Ingestion \& Automated Tissue QC:} Digital whole-slide images acquired from clinical optical scanners (Hamamatsu, Aperio CS2, 3DHISTECH) are tiled at native resolution. Automated quality control filters glass background, pen marks, air bubbles, and severe out-of-focus blur. Tiles with valid tissue fraction $\ge 0.10$ proceed to runtime inference.
\item \emph{Pre-Adaptation Inference \& Conformal Risk Scoring:} The frozen source model generates baseline predictions $y_0 = f_{\theta_0}(x)$ and non-conformity scores under the covariate-shift calibrated threshold $\hat{\lambda}_w$. If the prediction set is a singleton ($|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\lambda}_w}(x)| = 1$), the tile is certified low-risk and admitted to the test-time adaptation queue.
\item \emph{Trust-Region Optimization \& Active Safeguards:} Admitted tiles update normalization parameters under hard $L_\infty \le 0.05$ constraints. The dual CPU/GPU buffer maintains pre-step snapshots. In dense detection, the active CMR monitor audits hypercellular tumor nests ($\rho_{\mathrm{cell}} \ge 0.15$). Isolated anomalies trigger atomic rollback ($\theta_t \leftarrow \theta_{t-1}$); persistent collapse ($\ge 5$ hypercellular silence events) triggers latching global fallback to $\theta_0$.
\item \emph{Triage Routing to Pathologist Second-Read:} Verified high-confidence predictions pre-populate the clinical Laboratory Information System (LIS) and Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) for rapid diagnostic sign-off. Conversely, ambiguous tiles ($|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\lambda}_w}(x)| \ne 1$) or fallback events trigger an automated second-read flag, routing the whole-slide region directly to a board-certified pathologist for manual review.
\item \emph{WSI Boundary Isolation \& Permanent Audit Logging:} Upon completing each whole-slide image, the controller resets all adapted parameters and optimizer states to $\theta_0$, ensuring complete patient diagnostic independence. All telemetry events (admissions, rollbacks, fallbacks) are serialized into standard HL7 FHIR R4 \texttt{Observation} resources (Code \texttt{TTA-ROLLBACK}, \texttt{TTA-FALLBACK}) and DICOM Part 18 audit records (SOP Class UID \texttt{1.2.840.10008.5.1.4.1.1.88.67}). Telemetry benchmarks confirm that failure interlock revocation occurs within $1.8$\,ms upon anomaly detection, with asynchronous LIS event dispatch completed in $<15$\,ms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Target Cohort Size and Geographic Diversity:} While patch volume is substantial ($N=85{,}054$), target Hospital~2 comprises 10 WSIs from 9 patients. Post-hoc power analysis establishes that $N=9$ provides statistical power $1 - \beta = 0.992$ at $\alpha = 0.01$ to confirm Tent's catastrophic collapse ($88.0\%$ to $50.0\%$), but prospective non-inferiority trials demonstrating narrow staging equivalence ($\delta = \pm 0.02$) require $N_{\text{req}} = 142$ patients across multi-ethnic global cohorts.
\item \textbf{Stream Sequence Dependence:} While episodic boundary controllers establish permutation invariance across patient order, historical continuous stream experiments reflect a single sequential ordering without intermediate resets.
\item \textbf{Exploratory Detection:} The Stage H4 tissue threshold amendment and ground-truth inspection classify detection findings as exploratory rather than confirmatory.
\item \textbf{Compute Hardware Heterogeneity:} Classification was evaluated on dedicated high-memory nodes (NVIDIA Tesla V100 and RTX 3090), whereas detection adaptation was executed on a Kaggle dedicated container with an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (14.56 GB VRAM).
\item \textbf{Clinical Environment Validation:} While SafeAdapt implements native HL7 FHIR R4 and DICOM Part 18 logging interfaces, formal clinical deployment requires end-to-end integration with live hospital Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Selective test-time adaptation and fail-closed runtime monitoring provide critical safeguards against catastrophic model collapse under histopathology domain shifts.
In multicenter patch classification, selective gating eliminates Tent collapse and reduces harmful adaptation by $>25\times$, while Optimal Transport Prototype Alignment (OT-TTA) reverses negative transfer on histochemical outliers ($0.9360$ on Slide~29; $0.9364$ cohort macro-AUROC).
In dense mitotic figure detection, the Cellularity Mitosis Ratio (CMR) eliminates false silence alarms on benign stroma, achieving positive transfer across the full 1,100-patch benchmark ($F_1 = 0.4891$).
By unifying pre-adaptation conformal risk control, trust-region parameter projection, and atomic state rollbacks with deterministic per-WSI episodic resets, SafeAdapt establishes a verified fail-closed medical interlock that bounds parameter drift and preserves patient-level diagnostic staging under severe domain shift.

\section{Formal Derivation and Step-by-Step Proof of Theorem 1}
\label{app:theorem1_proof}
\begin{proof}
Let feature extractor $\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ produce representations satisfying Assumption 1: $\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \|\phi(x)\|_2 \le R_\phi < \infty$.
Let the linear classification head be parameterized by weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 1}$ and bias $b \in \mathbb{R}$, yielding logit $z(x) = W^T \phi(x) + b$ with operator norm $\|W\|_2 \le L_W$ (Assumption 2).
Under test-time adaptation across stream $t \in [1, T]$, affine parameters $\theta = (\gamma, \beta)$ are updated.
For any target input $x$, let $\phi_0(x)$ denote the feature representation under source parameters $\theta_0$, and $\phi_T(x)$ denote the representation under adapted parameters $\theta_T$.
By the enforced $L_\infty$ trust region constraint, $\|\theta_T - \theta_0\|_\infty \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}$.
Because BatchNorm normalizes features coordinate-wise, the parameter displacement induces a feature representation perturbation satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\|\phi_T(x) - \phi_0(x)\|_2 \le \|\theta_T - \theta_0\|_\infty \|\phi_0(x)\|_2 \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}} R_\phi.
\end{equation}
Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the linear logit difference yields:
\begin{align}
|z_T(x) - z_0(x)| &= |W^T (\phi_T(x) - \phi_0(x))| \notag \\
&\le \|W\|_2 \|\phi_T(x) - \phi_0(x)\|_2 \le L_W R_\phi \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{align}
The output probability is given by the sigmoid function $p(x) = \sigma(z(x)) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z(x)}}$.
By the Mean Value Theorem, there exists $\xi$ between $z_0(x)$ and $z_T(x)$ such that:
\begin{equation}
|\sigma(z_T(x)) - \sigma(z_0(x))| = |\sigma'(\xi)| \cdot |z_T(x) - z_0(x)|.
\end{equation}
Since $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$, the derivative achieves its global maximum at $\sigma(z) = 0.5$, yielding $\sup_{z \in \mathbb{R}} |\sigma'(z)| = \frac{1}{4}$.
Substituting the logit perturbation bound into the sigmoid derivative bound gives:
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} |\sigma(z_T(x)) - \sigma(z_0(x))| \le \frac{1}{4} L_W R_\phi \epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{equation}
For empirical source weights, $\|W_{\mathrm{head}}\|_2 = 1.8421$, normalized feature ball radius $R_\phi \le 2.0$, and trust region radius $\epsilon_{\mathrm{trust}} = 0.05$.
Evaluating the numerical bound yields:
\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{\max} \le \frac{1}{4} (1.8421) (2.0) (0.05) = 0.04605 \approx 0.0460.
\end{equation}
For any target sample whose baseline prediction margin satisfies $|p_0(x) - 0.5| > \Delta p_{\max} = 0.0460$, the output probability cannot cross the classification threshold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $p_0(x) > 0.5460$, then $p_T(x) \ge p_0(x) - \Delta p_{\max} > 0.5000$.
\item If $p_0(x) < 0.4540$, then $p_T(x) \le p_0(x) + \Delta p_{\max} < 0.5000$.
\end{enumerate}
Because samples outside the $\pm 0.0460$ margin cannot cross the decision threshold, global single-class attractor collapse ($p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$) is mathematically precluded for these samples across the full adaptation trajectory.
We reiterate that this bound strictly governs head output perturbations under parameter trust-region projection, and does not replace empirical clinical validation under severe biological or optical distribution shifts.
\end{proof}
